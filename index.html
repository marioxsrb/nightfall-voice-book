Caregiver leads the sentence. Sage stands behind it with the science. Drop the Sage and it reads like a wellness app. Drop the Caregiver and it reads like a lab report.

Three decisions for the team

The ratioSet here at 70 to 30. Moving science to the front changes the whole lexicon.
ApneaFear stays out. Apnea may deserve an exception, said calmly and once.
The word alphaIt is engineering language and it cools the tone. A warmer word may serve better.

What we checked

Research behind the hooks, August 2026.

Numbers sellEight Sleep opens ads on a figure, "up to 34% more deep sleep", and names the study behind it.
Oura hooksMost of Oura's ad library opens in the first person, on camera, with a result, not a feature list.
The competitionRISE runs a sleep clinic over telehealth and leads with "Done with sleep hacks? Try science", no referral, insurance accepted.
OrthosomniaChasing a perfect score raises sleep anxiety. Named by sleep researchers in 2017 and now common in press coverage.
The waitPatients report two to three months for a sleep clinic appointment. Online specialists book within days.
Search volume"Why can't I sleep" is the top sleep question in 46 states. "Why am I so tired" gets 74,000 searches a month worldwide.
What makes a hook workUGC research for 2026 puts specificity above cleverness. Every winning hook carries one constraint: a time, a cost, a device, or a "without".
Three seconds71% of viewers decide whether to keep watching inside three seconds, on average after 1.7.
Warning hooks"Stop doing this" openers beat positive ones on TikTok. Rule 7 keeps us out of that lane, so we buy the attention with numbers instead.
