What we checked

Research behind the hooks, August 2026.

Numbers sellEight Sleep opens ads on a figure, "up to 34% more deep sleep", and names the study behind it.
Oura hooksMost of Oura's ad library opens in the first person, on camera, with a result inside the first two seconds.
The competitionRISE runs a sleep clinic over telehealth and leads with "Done with sleep hacks? Try science", self-booking, insurance accepted.
OrthosomniaChasing a perfect score raises sleep anxiety. Named by sleep researchers in 2017 and now common in press coverage.
The waitPatients report two to three months for a sleep clinic appointment. Online specialists book within days.
Oura, verbatim"This tiny little ring here has completely changed the game for me." "Sleep is the single most important metric in your life." First person, on camera, 57 seconds on average, and some creatives keep running past 1,500 days.
Oura, 2026The Subtle Power campaign and the World Cup spot "World's Smallest Ring", which pulled 267 times the engagement of the median World Cup ad.
Whoop, verbatim"Unlock your best performance." "The Best Obsess." Sign-off on everything: "See where WHOOP can take you." Their ads score highest on competence and achievement.
HormoziThe hook carries about 80% of an ad. Call out the exact person, name the goal, give it a deadline. Write it so the reader thinks: that happened to me.
Headlines with numbersHeadlines carrying a number earn around 36% more clicks. This is why F2 exists.
Search volumeOne question tops sleep search in 46 states: why sleep keeps failing. "Why am I so tired" gets 74,000 searches a month worldwide.
What makes a hook workUGC research for 2026 puts specificity above cleverness. Every winning hook carries one constraint: a time, a cost, a device, or a limit.
Three seconds71% of viewers decide whether to keep watching inside three seconds, on average after 1.7.
Warning hooks"Stop doing this" openers beat positive ones on TikTok. Rule 7 keeps us out of that lane, so we buy the attention with numbers instead.
